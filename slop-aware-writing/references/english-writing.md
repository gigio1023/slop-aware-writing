# English Writing Layer

Load this file only when an English-specific slop candidate survives the common diagnosis. It is not an English grammar or style guide. Use the model's ordinary fluency for syntax and idiom, and prefer the publication's style guide, the writer's repeated voice, and the document's genre.

## Diagnose before editing

1. Identify the English variety, register, and reader when the proposed edit depends on them. Do not normalize an established variety into an unstated default.
2. Note deliberate voice signals: contractions, first person, fragments, technical density, humor, profanity, asides, and sentence rhythm.
3. Fix meaning, evidence, relevance, and reader context before surface habits.
4. Treat one fashionable word or punctuation mark as coincidence. Act on a recurring pattern that harms the target text.

## English-specific slop lenses

**Delayed point.** Openers such as "here's the thing", "let's be clear", and "it is important to note" often postpone the clause that carries information. Cut the opener when nothing is lost. Keep a personal aside that adds genuine context or character.

**Padded verb phrase.** Prefer the direct verb when phrases such as "serves as", "has the ability to", "makes a decision", or "provides an explanation of" add no distinction. Keep the longer form when it expresses aspect, capability, formality, or a defined relationship that the plain verb would erase.

**Inflated significance.** Remove claims that something is pivotal, profound, transformative, a testament, or a cornerstone unless the document establishes the consequence. Replace only with supported scope or effect.

**Insider performance.** Openers such as "what nobody tells you" and "the part everyone misses" manufacture authority. State the supported point without claiming universal ignorance.

**Withheld conclusion.** Repeated "not X, not Y, but Z", self-answered questions, colon reveals, and stacked fragments can delay the claim. State the conclusion once unless the contrast corrects a real misconception or belongs to the writer's established voice.

**Participial afterthought.** An `-ing` clause at the end of a sentence often asserts an unproved result or causal relation. Keep it if the subject and relation are unambiguous. Otherwise make the relation explicit, split the sentence, or remove the unsupported analysis.

**Mechanical closure.** Remove conclusion labels, recap paragraphs, future outlook sections, and aphoristic kickers that add no decision or action. Keep a conclusion when the genre expects synthesis and the synthesis does new work.

**Synonym display.** Repeat a precise noun instead of rotating among `agent`, `assistant`, `system`, and `tool`. Variety is not worth referential ambiguity.

## Fluency boundary

Do not turn contractions, active voice, short words, parallel grammar, em dashes, title case, semicolons, or one prestige dialect into a human-sounding recipe. The model already knows how these forms work. Change one only when it participates in the diagnosed slop pattern or conflicts with a governing style. Preserve qualifiers that carry uncertainty, frequency, scope, or politeness.

## Keep test

Before applying a rule, ask:

- Does the edit preserve the claim's force, logical relation, and attribution?
- Does this construction recur as part of the writer's voice?
- Is it normal or required in this English variety, genre, or domain?
- Does the new version sound more specific only because it invented detail?

When the first three answers argue for keeping the span, keep it. When the last answer is yes, revert the invented specificity.

## Evidence limits

English anti-slop projects provide useful hypotheses, but word bans and active-voice rules often encode one author's taste. Research on model-assisted revision supports checking for voice drift, not reconstructing an author's identity from surface markers. Use the writer's own samples and minimal edits.
